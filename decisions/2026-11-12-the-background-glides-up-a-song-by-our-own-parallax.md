## 2026-11-12 — The background glides up a song by our own parallax, not reveal.js's

**Context:** With a background picture of one's own in place (see [each reader may put a picture of their own behind the slides](2026-11-12-each-reader-may-put-a-picture-of-their-own-behind-the-slides.md)), the question was whether reveal.js's parallax background could move it along as one pages.

**Decision:** Yes, as an option in ⚙️, „Hintergrund-Bewegung (Parallax) aktivieren", off unless checked. The picture, as wide as the screen and as tall as its proportions make it (at least three tenths taller than the screen), glides up as one goes down a song's slides, all the way from its top edge to its bottom edge, with reveal.js's own timing (0.8 s, as the slide moves), and never sideways. Another song, or the table of contents, starts at the top again, by a crossfade rather than a sweep back up: a second copy of the picture, already there, fades in over the first. It is our own code (`placePicture` in `body-controls.html`, a `transform` in `night.css`), not reveal.js's `parallaxBackgroundImage`. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` it neither glides nor fades.

**Reasoning:** reveal.js's `parallaxBackgroundImage` would have barely moved: it divides the picture's spare height by every vertical slide of the deck at once (`getVerticalSlides()` returns all of them, over 600 here), not the current song's, so a step down a song moved it by a pixel or two. It also wants the picture's size in pixels, which a picture covering screens of every shape and a phone turned sideways would have to recompute on every resize, animates `background-position`, which repaints a screenful of picture in every frame, and sits on reveal.js's `.backgrounds` element, where our veil would have needed a layer of its own on top. Across the deck, as reveal.js does it, a picture a fifth wider than the screen moved by a quarter of a percent of its width a song, with 78 songs. Three ways were tried out live: across and down each song („pro Lied diagonal"), across a run of ten songs with a crossfade between runs („Zehnerblöcke"), and down each song only („nur senkrecht"). Down only won: the book is mostly paged through the table of contents, from which a song's place in the deck says nothing, so movement across songs was mostly seen as a crossfade anyway, while going down a song is what one does while playing. Its timing went full circle: reveal.js's own 0.8 s first; then, asked for slower and smoother, 2 s, setting off gently and trailing behind the slide; and back to reveal.js's, once a picture in portrait travelled its whole height and the slow glide over that long way felt like wading through treacle. A fixed room of three tenths of the screen's height was tried first, for every picture alike; but a picture in portrait, cut to that, lost most of itself and moved no more than a landscape one, when it is the one with the most height to show. So the picture is shown whole across, as tall as that makes it, and travels its full height over every song: shown as `<img>`s, whose proportions set their height without the picture's size having to be stored or measured. A song with few slides then takes long steps, one with many short ones. A crossfade back to the top, since a sweep back up across the whole picture in one go is the one movement that draws the eye; the table of contents and a song's title slide both sit at the top, so the most frequent jump changes nothing. Off unless checked, since moving backgrounds don't suit every reader.
