## 2026-10-28 — A title takes as many lines as make it largest

**Context:** On an iPad in landscape, „Can't Help Falling in Love" sat on one line from edge to edge while three quarters of the screen's height stayed empty. A title only wrapped where it was too long for the window, and then greedily, at the window's width ([a wrapped title is fitted by its widest line](2026-10-28-a-wrapped-title-is-fitted-by-its-widest-line.md) fixed how such a title is measured, not where it breaks). Split over two or three lines, the same title fits far larger.

**Decision:** `slide-zoom.js` chooses each title slide's line count. It measures the heading's words once on a single line, works out the narrowest split into each number of lines from where they stand, predicts the scale each split would fit at, and sets the winning split's width as the heading's `max-width`. `shared.css` centres the heading and balances its lines. Measured over the whole book, the median title grows ×1.4 on an iPad in landscape, ×1.7 in portrait and ×1.1–1.2 on a desktop, and none gets smaller on any screen. Lyrics are left alone.

**Reasoning:** A fixed line length in CSS (`max-width` in `em`, with `text-wrap: balance`) was measured first, at 6–10em. It cannot know the screen's shape. On a phone held sideways, a second or third line costs more height than it saves width, and titles shrank to 0.64×. On a wide desktop, short titles like „Stand By Me" got smaller split in two. `text-wrap: balance` alone changed almost nothing, because wide screens kept nearly every title on one line. The right count depends on both the title and the screen, so it is chosen per title, and one line is always among the candidates, which is why no title can come out smaller. Measuring the words on one line, rather than laying the heading out once per candidate, keeps it to one extra layout per batch. Because rewrapping changes the content's size after the ResizeObserver reported it, Chrome raises „ResizeObserver loop completed with undelivered notifications". A rewrapped content is therefore unobserved and observed again from the next frame. That frame's fit finds nothing to change, because the choice depends only on the words and the room, not on the current wrapping. Lyrics never wrap: a line of lyrics is a musical phrase with its chords placed inside it, and breaking it would split the phrase and scatter the chords. Not addressed here: where a title fills the height on a touch screen, its first or last line can reach the corner buttons (📖 over „Somewhere" on a phone held sideways). Tall lyric slides already did this before, and keeping the corners clear is a separate change.
