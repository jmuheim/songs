## 2026-10-04 — The presenter must not self-release while following its own delegate

**Context:** Field testing surfaced a recurring problem: delegating scrolling to a guest usually worked, but the presenter often lost the ability to end the session afterwards — the ❌ overlay on 🚀 would vanish, or 🚀 would read „Live-Scrollen beenden" but clicking it did nothing, forcing a reload to recover. Tracing it: once the presenter hands off to a guest, its own tab sets `navMode = 'follower'` (see `applyNavMode`) — it is not scrolling, just watching the guest like any other audience member. The periodic release-checker frees *any* follower that hasn't heard from the session in three heartbeats (~6s), so a brief connection blip on the *guest's* side — plausible on venue wifi — silently called `releaseSilently()` on the presenter's own tab: `session` went `null`, the ❌ disappeared, 🚀 reverted to „Live-Scrollen starten" — while the guest, oblivious, kept scrolling, and everyone else stayed locked to a session the presenter could no longer see, let alone end.

**Decision:** The release-checker's `setInterval` in `body-controls.html` now excludes the presenter: `!isPresenter` was added to the condition that calls `releaseSilently()`. A presenter that delegated to a guest stays locked into (and in authority over) that session no matter how long the guest's connection stays dark; only 🚀 → „Live-Scrollen beenden" ends it.

**Reasoning:** The self-release exists so an ordinary follower — who has no other way back to free navigation — doesn't freeze forever if the presenter disappears. The presenter is categorically different: it is the one actor with a deliberate, always-available way to end the session, so silently stripping that away on a timeout serves no one — it just turns a recoverable connection hiccup into a stuck room requiring a manual reload. Exempting it is a one-line, low-risk fix because every other consequence of being a 'follower' (locked keyboard/touch/controls, snapping to `lastState`) is harmless to leave in place for the presenter too: it simply sits still, view-wise, until the guest's heartbeat resumes or the presenter ends the session outright. Tested in [index_html_spec.rb](../spec/browser/index_html_spec.rb) with a phantom guest that volunteers and then never sends another message — the presenter must stay `session-active` and successfully end the session well past the three-heartbeat window. See also [ending a session now repeats its broadcast](2026-10-04-ending-a-session-repeats-its-broadcast-to-survive-one-dropped-packet.md), the companion fix for the same field-testing report.
