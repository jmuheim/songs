## 2026-10-28 — Each song slide previews the next slide's first line

**Context:** Whoever scrolls a song (the presenter or a guest in a live-scroll session) is often a little late, so the room sings the first line of the next section before it is on screen. They needed to see what comes next while the current slide is still up.

**Decision:**
- **What:** every sung sub-slide of a song (`section.level2`, but not the Infos slide) ends on a `<p class="next-line">`: „Next:", the next sub-slide's heading, „-" and its first line, chords included, then „..." („Next: **Chorus** - I was the [Am] one who let you [C] know..."). A song's last sub-slide says „Ende des Lieds" instead.
- **Not on:** the song's own title slide or its „Infos über das Lied" slide (nothing is sung there yet; the user asked for the Infos slide to go without), or the Introduction (not a song). `print.html` gets none either.
- **Built, not scripted:** `inject_next_line_previews` in `lib/build_helpers.rb` adds it at build time, before `wrap_slide_content`. The "first line" is the next slide's first block after its heading, up to the first `<br>`.
- **Placed under the lyrics, on one line:** it sits inside `.slide-content`, so `slide-zoom.js` fits it together with the lyrics, at 0.6em under a rule. It never wraps: where it is wider than the lyrics, the slide is fitted to it.
- **Dimmed by opacity alone:** the preview keeps the lyrics' colours, and its heading (bold) keeps the slide titles' colour. It is set apart by its size, the rule above it, and an opacity that never gets past 60%.
- **Fading in late:** the preview is fully transparent when its slide becomes the current one, and after 3 s it fades in slowly to 60% over another 3 s. Every visit starts over. Leaving, it keeps its opacity until Reveal's 0.8 s slide transition is over, and only then is it reset. A chord in it that takes the focus shows it at once.
- **Ordinary content:** screen readers read it, and its chords are chord buttons like any other (see [chords are real button elements](2026-10-28-chords-are-real-button-elements.md)), fingering included. `chords.js` sizes that fingering as if the chord were set at the lyrics' size, not at the preview's 0.6em.

**Reasoning:**
- **Under the lyrics, not pinned to the screen bottom:** this was the user's choice. Inside the fitted content it can never overlap the lyrics, and it needs no change to `slide-zoom.js`. A strip pinned to the screen edge would have kept a fixed size, but the fit would then have had to reserve its height, like the chevron gutters.
- **One line, at the cost of size:** the first version wrapped the preview within the lyrics' width (`contain: inline-size`), so a short slide (an Intro of four chords) kept its large fit. The user wanted the line unbroken, to read at a glance like the lyrics. So a short slide now gets smaller: the first fixture song's Intro went from scale 3.57 without a preview to 1.90 with it (2.63 when it wrapped).
- **„Next:", the heading and „...":** the user's choice too, wording included. „Chorus" says which part comes, which helps when the first line alone (often just chords) doesn't. „Next:" tells the eye, and a screen reader, that the line is not part of the current slide, and „..." that the section goes on past it.
- **Why it fades in late:** the user asked for it. The preview is only needed toward the end of a slide; at its start it would only pull the eye away from the lines being sung. A slow fade arrives without a jolt. The timings (3 s wait, 3 s fade) were a pick for "a few seconds" and "slowly", in one place in `shared.css`.
- **Why transitions, not an animation:** the first version faded in with a CSS animation tied to `.present`. When the slide stopped being current, the animation ended with the class, and the preview snapped to transparent while the old slide was still moving out. The user asked that it not disappear abruptly. Transitions can treat the two directions differently: the slow fade-in after a wait, and on leaving a hold as long as Reveal's own slide transition, which fades the whole slide out anyway. A transition needs an earlier value to start from, which a slide shown straight from `display: none` doesn't have; `@starting-style` supplies the transparent start there. A browser without it (Safari before 17.5) just shows that preview at once.
- **Why no colours of its own:** the first version greyed the text. The user asked for no font colour changes: the preview should read like the lyrics it previews, only quieter, and its opacity dims text and chords alike (first 80%, then 60% at the user's request). Its heading briefly lost the title colour too; the user wanted that back, so the heading reads like the slide title it will become.
- **Why `opacity` and `:focus-within`, not `visibility`:** at `opacity: 0` the preview stays in the accessibility tree, so a screen reader can read ahead from the first moment, and its chords can be reached. A chord focused while the preview is transparent would carry an invisible ring, so focus shows the whole preview at once.
- **At build time, like the TOC filter:** the markup is static, the golden fixtures pin it, and a unit spec covers the line extraction. A script would have had to rebuild the same thing in every browser on every load.
- **Rejected, `aria-hidden` and copies-only chords:** the first version hid the preview from screen readers ("it only repeats the next slide") and therefore kept its chords out of the tab order and without fingerings. But a follower in a live-scroll session cannot move on to read ahead, and reveal.js keeps every other slide `inert`. A blind singer is late in the same way as everyone else, so the preview is for them too. The extra tab stops are acceptable: whoever doesn't want them hides the chords in ⚙️.
- **Fingering at the lyrics' size:** sized from the preview chord's own 0.6em, the fingering came out at 60% of the next slide's. On a phone that is too small to read.
