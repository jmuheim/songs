## 2026-10-31 — Each evening is its own session, led by a host who logs in

**Context:** Every deploy had one multiplex channel, its pair baked into the build ([the multiplex token is generated locally](2026-10-01-multiplex-token-is-generated-locally.md)). So everybody who opened songs.josh.ch was in every live-scroll session: someone browsing the book at home was locked to a stranger's evening, and two evenings at the same time pushed each other's presenter out through the claim rule. 🚀 also sat in every visitor's 📡 dialog, though only the one leading the evening ever needs it. axipedia, which inspired the role model, has the same one-channel-per-deploy setup, so it offered no answer to this part.

**Decision:**
- **A session is one evening, and each device keeps its secret in its own storage.** „Als Gastgeber anmelden" (📡 dialog, password once per device) generates a fresh secret in the browser. Every device derives the relay's channel from it, `socketId = sha256(secret)`. A device in no session opens no socket at all. The build carries no pair any more: `multiplex-token.json`, `rake multiplex:token` and the CI step that minted a pair are gone.
- **The QR code carries the secret, and the address only brings it in.** 🔗 is offered to the host and to everyone already in the session, and its code is `?live=<secret>&qr=1`, so whoever sits next to a latecomer can let them in. A plain visitor has no 🔗. The scanning device stores the secret (`localStorage`) and strips `?live=` from its address at once.
- **A session expires after 12 hours of silence,** with no message heard and no heartbeat sent, and a guest can leave with „Session verlassen".
- **One host at a time; the role is taken over, not handed on.** In a session, the login button reads „Gastgeber übernehmen". Whoever logs in last leads, and the previous host steps down when it hears the higher claim. It stays in the session as an ordinary participant. A live scroll that is running carries on: whoever scrolls keeps scrolling, and the new host can end it. There is no co-host and no „Gastgeber abgeben".
- **The host repeats itself for as long as it hosts,** not only while a song is live-scrolled: every heartbeat says who hosts (the claim) and what is live-scrolled, if anything (`live`, or `null`).
- **„Session beenden"** (host only) tells every device to leave: they forget the session, the socket closes, and anyone who was live-scrolling lands on the table of contents.

**Reasoning:**
- **The secret, not only the socketId, has to travel.** Guests send too: a volunteer's „Ja", and then the guest scroller's position. The relay forwards nothing without the secret, so a QR code with only the socketId would have broken guest scrolling. Having the secret now means you are in the session. Before, anybody could read it from the page source.
- **Stored on the device, not kept in the address.** The first version kept `?live=` in the address for as long as a device was in the session. That had two problems:
  - Reveal writes the address at most once a second, and a write it deferred carries the query string of the moment it was asked for. A login in that second lost `?live=` again. In practice that is unlikely, since typing a password takes longer, but the fix was a second, delayed write of our own, and that was fragile.
  - A song book reopened from the home screen starts at the address it was added with. A session in the address was lost on every such restart, or, when the book was added during a session, kept there for good.

  In storage, a session outlasts a reload, a new tab and a restart from the home screen, and the address stays plain. The price: being in a session is per device, not per tab. That is harmless, since two tabs show the same anyway. The sender id stays per tab, so two tabs are still two senders, and a host reopened under a new id names itself scroller again.
- **Twelve hours,** because storage never forgets: without an expiry, a guest would still be in last week's session, and a host who simply went home would still be its host. A running evening renews it all the time. „Session verlassen" gives a guest a way out before that.
- **Generated in the browser, not fetched from the relay's `/token`.** `/token` does exactly `randomBytes(16)` plus SHA-256, and every device has to compute the hash from the secret anyway. Generating locally keeps logging in free of a request to Railway, and keeps the earlier rule that the pair is never fetched.
- **A small SHA-256 of our own (`style/sha256.js`), not `crypto.subtle`.** `crypto.subtle` is asynchronous and only exists in a secure context. It would fail on a phone that reaches `./dev` over the LAN by IP address, and its promise would have threaded through the page's whole start-up.
- **Taking over beats handing over.** A host whose phone died cannot hand anything on, and a takeover needs nothing from the old host. The claim rule already settled two presenters. A co-host would have had nothing of their own to do, and two people pressing 🚀 or setting who is present would only get in each other's way. The claim of a takeover is above every claim heard in the session, not just above its own clock, so a phone whose clock is behind still wins. axipedia learned that the hard way.
- **A heartbeat that never stops** lets a host that was offline during a takeover find out within one beat that it has been replaced. It also carries the end of a live scroll on every beat. That replaces the end message's own repeats from [ending a session repeats its broadcast](2026-10-04-ending-a-session-repeats-its-broadcast-to-survive-one-dropped-packet.md), which only the end of the whole session still needs.
- **Two words.** „Session" now means the evening and „Live-Scrollen" one song. In the code, `session` was renamed to `live`, and the presenter became the host.
- **What storage can't fix:** on an iPhone, a song book added to the home screen keeps its storage apart from Safari's, and a scanned QR code always opens in Safari. A guest therefore joins in Safari. A host who logs in inside the home-screen app stays host there.
