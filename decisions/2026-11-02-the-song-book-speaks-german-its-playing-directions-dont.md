## 2026-11-02 — The song book speaks German, its playing directions don't

**Context:** With the domain lied.li registered (read: „Liedli"), the song book was to become German throughout. Its dialogs and toasts already were; still English were a few labels (📖, ⚙️, „Reset", „Next:"), the Introduction, the chord tooltip's „Guitar:", and in the songs the section headings (Chorus, Verse, …) and the resource links' labels (Source tab, Guitar tutorial, …).

**Decision:** Everything the book says itself is German: the controls, the Einleitung, the section headings (Strophe, Refrain, Vor-Refrain, Zwischenspiel, Überleitung, Mittelteil) and the link labels (Tab-Quelle, Gitarren-Tutorial). A session is a „Runde" on screen. The deck is called „Liedli 🔥🎶🌛", but only its title says so; elsewhere it's the „Liederbuch". Not translated: playing directions in the lyrics and the notes in `## Spielhinweise` (the source-only heading itself is German, it was `## Instructions`), the chord chart on the Einleitung, the chord names, and the words German musicians use anyway: Intro, Outro, Bridge, Solo, Instrumental, Capo, Tab, Live-Scrollen, OK.

**Reasoning:** Musicians who don't speak German play along, and directions are what they need to read; a heading they can tell from its place on the slide. „Bridge" over „Brücke": it's what musicians say in German too, and unambiguous for the others. „Runde" over „Sitzung", which sounds like a board meeting, and over keeping „Session". „Vor-Refrain" over the hybrid „Pre-Refrain". B stays B, not H: the German H was never liked here, the tabs from the web all say B, and the chord colours and fingerings key off the letter. „Liedli" is meant to be a little funny, which wears off with use, hence used sparingly. The domain itself moves separately, once lied.li is set up on the server; links and the deploy stay on songs.josh.ch until then.
