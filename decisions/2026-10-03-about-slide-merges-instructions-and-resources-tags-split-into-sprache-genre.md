## 2026-10-03 — About slide merges Instructions and Resources; tags split into Sprache/Genre

**Context:** Each song could carry up to three separate screen-only slides near the top — `## About` (tags), `## Instructions` (alternate chord fingerings, on some songs), and `## Resources` (links) at the very end. The request was to always show one combined slide at the front, and to offer the table-of-contents tag filter as two `<select>` dropdowns (Sprache, Genre) instead of a flat row of OR'd chips.

**Decision:** `merge_about_section` in `lib/build_helpers.rb` folds `## About`, `## Instructions` and `## Resources` into a single `## About` section at the front, at build time — the source `.md` files keep writing them as three separate sections, unchanged. The tags move into the same bullet list as the Resources links, each labelled by category (`- Sprache: Mundart`, `- Genre: Pop`) via a new `tag_category` classification (`LANGUAGE_TAGS` — everything else is a genre). `inject_toc_filter` uses the same classification to build two independent `<select>` dropdowns (plus a "Reset" button) instead of one row of toggle-chips; the two categories combine with **AND** (not the chips' OR), since they're now two different, independent dimensions rather than one flat tag list.

**Reasoning:** Keeping the merge at build time (not in the source files) means `tab-to-song` and manual editing keep the same three-section shape, which reads better in the source than one slide with mixed content would. AND instead of OR for Sprache×Genre is the natural reading of two separate dropdowns — a user picking "Englisch" and "Pop" expects *both*, not either; OR only made sense for the old flat, single-list chip bar. One side effect accepted as part of this: since the merged `## About` is still stripped wholesale from `print.html` (tags and links are screen-only), `## Instructions` content — previously visible in print — now disappears from the printed version too. This wasn't carved out with an exception because the request didn't ask for one and a printed alternate-fingering diagram is a minor loss; worth revisiting if it turns out to matter in practice.
