## 2026-11-26 — The host's hearts are its playlist, and „Nächstes Lied" follows it

**Context:** After an evening of singing, the user wanted to define a playlist in advance. The host should choose whether the guests may pick favourites with their hearts or only the host does. At the end of a song, a button should go to the next one on the list: the one with the most hearts when the guests may wish. The ❤️ already existed, as a per-session wish counted by every device (see [guests wish for songs](2026-11-11-guests-wish-for-songs-with-a-heart-counted-per-session.md)).

**Decision:**
- **The host's hearts are its playlist,** in the order they were set, and kept on its device (`localStorage` `playlist`) until it empties it, not with the session. Outside a session they show on a device the password was entered on, numbered „1.", „2.", …, so the playlist can be made days ahead. Wherever they mean the playlist (there, and on every device while the host alone makes it), they are a ⭐ instead of a ❤️.
- **🔥 › ⭐ „Playlist" says whether „Gäste dürfen Lieder wünschen".** If so (the default, as before), everyone's hearts count, the host's among them. If not, every device shows the host's playlist with its places, and the guests' hearts are disabled. The choice rides on the host's heartbeat, and is kept on the device for the next session it opens.
- **Played:** a song the live scroll stayed on the last sub-slide of for 5 seconds, or moved on from there to another song, as the host's deck saw it. The host keeps the list on its heartbeat. A played song keeps its heart, wears a ✓ and is not proposed again.
- **„Nächstes Lied: «…»"**, a button built below „Ende des Lieds" on every song's last sub-slide, goes to the next song. That is the next one on the playlist, or, while the guests wish, the most wished, the playlist's order and then the book's breaking a tie. It skips the song itself, the played ones, and those this tab already left by the button. Only whoever may navigate sees it: the scroller takes the room along, a follower has none.

**Reasoning:**
- **The hearts, not a list of its own.** The user chose the order the hearts were set over a sortable list, which would have needed drag handles or ↑↓ buttons on a phone. To move a song, the host takes its heart away and sets it again. The playlist then rides on the `wish` message every device already sends, so no new message type was needed: a guest knows which set is the host's by the heartbeat's sender.
- **On the device, not with the session.** The user chose this so that the playlist can be made days ahead. A session forgotten after twelve silent hours would have lost it, and the next evening would have started from nothing. The guests' hearts stay per session, as before: they answer what the room wants tonight.
- **A star, not a heart of another colour.** A playlist place is not a vote, and the guests' hearts beside it would have looked the same. The user ruled out a differently coloured heart, which the colour-blind would not tell apart, and picked the ⭐ from a star, a pin, a clover and a note: another shape, read as „marked".
- **Played by the live scroll's end, after a while, not by the button.** The user chose that a played song drops out of the list and keeps its mark. Reaching the last slide while live scrolling is when the room has sung it, but only once it stays there: a scroller who overshoots and goes back up has not ended the song. Five seconds there, or moving on from there to another song (by „Nächstes Lied" or 📖), count. Browsing doesn't count: a host may look a song up without playing it. The button also remembers, per tab, which songs it was pressed on. Where nothing is played (outside a session, or browsing in one), two songs at the top would otherwise send the reader back and forth between them.
- **A playlist dialog of its own.** The checkbox and „Playlist leeren" first sat in the 🔥 dialog. There they would have added two rows to a dialog that already nearly fills a phone held sideways. One ⭐ button costs one row, and its dialog has room to say what the two choices mean. Measured in a real browser, even that row was too many: the host's 🔥 dialog had already run past the bottom of a laptop's low window (with 🚀's hint, on the table of contents) and of a phone held sideways. There it is now set smaller, and on the phone wider, its buttons two to a row; `mobile_spec.rb` now opens it as the host sees it too.
