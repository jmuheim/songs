## 2026-10-04 — Connection-status toasts layer on top of the persistent status line, not instead of it

**Context:** Following the field-testing fixes for the presenter losing its ability to end a session ([the presenter must not self-release](2026-10-04-presenter-must-not-self-release-while-following-its-own-delegate.md)), the request was to give people more insight into the multiplex connection state generally — "connection lost" and similar — via toasts, so a stale or locked view has an explanation instead of just silently sitting there.

**Decision:** `#multiplex-toast` gets four new one-off messages, each gated on `session` (nobody cares about connectivity while just browsing freely) and each firing only on a *transition*, never repeated while the condition persists: „Verbindung verloren" / „Wieder verbunden" on the socket's own `disconnect`/`connect` events, and — presenter only — „Keine Rückmeldung vom Gast mehr …" / „Der Gast ist wieder verbunden" when the delegated guest's heartbeat goes stale and recovers (the same `lastSessionAt` staleness check that already drives follower self-release, now also read — not acted on — for the exempted presenter). `#multiplex-status`'s existing "Keine Verbindung" text is untouched.

**Reasoning:** The status line already correctly reports *ongoing* state for as long as it holds — a toast for that would either have to persist indefinitely (unlike every other toast in the app, all of which fade) or re-fire on a timer, both worse than just leaving the status line alone. Toasts are the right tool specifically for the *edges*: the moment something changes is exactly when a brief, attention-grabbing notice earns its keep, and exactly what the status line's quiet, easy-to-miss text under the top-right buttons doesn't provide. Gating on `session` mirrors the status line's own existing gating (`everConnected && session` for "Keine Verbindung") for the same reason: a drop while nobody is relying on the connection for anything is not worth interrupting anyone over.

One test-infrastructure trap surfaced while covering the guest-silence pair: the release-checker's `setInterval` ticks on a fixed 500ms, never scaled by `cfg.heartbeat` (only the *threshold*, `3 * cfg.heartbeat`, is configurable). A spec using `heartbeat: 100` to reach "stale" quickly produces a 300ms threshold — shorter than the 500ms tick — so "fresh again" could never be observed: by the time the next tick ran, more than 300ms had always already passed since even a perfectly timed recovery. `heartbeat: 300` (900ms threshold, comfortably above the 500ms tick) fixed it; see the comment left on the checker itself in `body-controls.html` and the spec in [index_html_spec.rb](../spec/browser/index_html_spec.rb).
